# supplement-sales-analysis
### Project Overview 
This project analyses weekly supplement sales data across Amazon, Walmart, and iHerb to understand how discounts, product categories, and locations impact revenue and return rates.
### Objective
- Identify sales trends over time
- Evaluate the impact of discounts on revenue
- Compare performance across platforms and regions
- Analyse product return rates
### Tools
- Google Sheets
- Tableau
### Key Inshights
- Revenue and sales remained relatively stable between 2020 and 2025, with several strong seasonal peaks observed throughout the dataset. Notable revenue spikes occurred in May 2023, September 2024, and December 2024, suggesting periods of increased consumer demand in the supplement market.
- Total revenue across all platforms reached approximately $22.9 million, generated from over 658,000 units sold, indicating strong and consistent e-commerce performance throughout the analysed period.
- Discount levels showed minimal impact on average revenue performance. While discount ranges between 0–20% maintained similar average unit sales and revenue values, discounts above 20% did not significantly improve revenue generation. This suggests that aggressive discounting may increase sales volume only marginally without producing proportional revenue growth.
- Amazon generated high overall revenue and sales volume but also recorded the highest average return rate at 1.04%, indicating potentially higher operational or customer satisfaction risks compared to other platforms.
- iHerb produced the highest total revenue overall at approximately $7.86 million while maintaining a relatively low return rate of 1.02%, demonstrating strong platform efficiency and stable customer purchasing behaviour.
- Walmart showed the lowest return rate at 1.00%, suggesting stronger order retention and fewer product returns despite generating slightly lower total revenue compared to competing platforms.
- Product categories displayed relatively consistent discount strategies, with average discounts ranging between 11.9% and 12.9% across all supplement types. This indicates standardised promotional pricing across the business.
- The Hydration category recorded the highest average return rate at 1.07%, while Herbal supplements showed the lowest at 0.94%, suggesting differences in customer satisfaction or product expectations between categories.
- Vitamin and Mineral products appeared most frequently within the dataset, indicating strong product availability and potentially higher consumer demand compared to smaller categories such as Omega or Amino Acids.
### Conclusion
This analysis demonstrates that the supplement e-commerce market maintained stable growth and consistent consumer demand between 2020 and 2025 across major online platforms. Although discounting strategies were commonly applied, higher discounts did not substantially improve average revenue performance, suggesting that excessive promotional pricing may reduce profitability without significantly increasing business returns.

Platform analysis showed that iHerb achieved the strongest revenue performance overall, while Walmart maintained the lowest return rates, highlighting differences in operational efficiency and customer purchasing behaviour across platforms. Additionally, category-level analysis revealed that certain product groups experience higher return risks, providing opportunities for businesses to improve product targeting, quality control, and customer satisfaction strategies.

Overall, the project highlights how sales trends, discount strategies, platform performance, and return-rate analysis can be used together to support data-driven business decisions within the supplement e-commerce industry.
